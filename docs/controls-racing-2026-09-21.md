# Controls, guided flight, teleport and cabinet racing

On foot, walking no longer rotates the camera automatically. Movement follows the manually chosen view every frame. The camera-controls integration now unwraps azimuth at +/-180 degrees and avoids a second angular spring; the library still handles camera clearance and dolly behavior. Vehicle chase view retains gentle recentering. Joystick outer travel runs automatically with threshold hysteresis, and jumping preserves released-stick momentum while retaining deliberate air steering.

The jetpack course has thicker 11.4 m rings, stronger contrast, three visible upcoming rings, and a screen-space next-ring/distance marker. Guidance defaults on and follows the next ring while forward input is held, including height adjustment. It can be toggled off for manual flight. Normal speed and boost are reduced, stopping is quicker, and the collection radius matches the larger openings. The timer starts when movement starts.

Play > Click to teleport (or T) arms a tap on a visible floor or path; dragging still looks around. Escape or Cancel disarms it. The expanded grounds map also accepts clicks on open ground. Landing checks cover boundaries, walls, people, the cart, the pool, and baked walking height. Visible-surface rays reject walls and elevated non-walkable surfaces. Teleporting is unavailable during a challenge; selecting it from Play first exits the challenge.

Cabinet Grand Prix is a fictional two-lap race against the existing game representations of Marco Rubio, Scott Bessent, and Doug Burgum. It uses a temporary 672 m estate circuit through the ballroom frame, around the north lawn and behind the West Wing, with sixteen ordered gates per lap, a countdown, live standings, seated drivers, rolling wheels, recovery with three seconds added to the clock, and final placing. AI carts follow the circuit and yield/change lanes near the player; soft contact separates carts rather than trapping them in an overlapping collision. Ordinary world collision still applies. Loading failure returns to free roam with a retry message. Exit, restart, map travel, pause and disposal clear or suspend the correct race state.

The race reuses the existing cart geometry/materials and cached staff assets. Only tint materials, labels, the temporary track and course effects are newly allocated; shared original model resources remain owned by their original loaders. Driver skeletons are independent.

Validation: real camera-controls solver across both angle-wrap directions; reverse-walking camera stability; simultaneous movement/jump/turn and joystick running; valid/invalid teleport and mesh occlusion; full guided eight-ring course (38 simulated seconds); real shipped cart and staff models with seat alignment checks; countdown, pause, anti-skip, recovery and complete two-lap driving simulation (63.5 simulated seconds, first place, six jumps, twelve item pickups/uses, 22 m/s peak boosted speed); original traversal/camera, world, vehicle, sandbox and render-budget regressions; TypeScript and production build. No browser visual review or real-device FPS measurement was performed.

The race HUD is collapsed by default on all devices: rank, lap, time and boost remain visible, while standings/restart/recovery sit behind the arrow. On touchscreens it shares the top row with the minimap, leaving the central driving view clear. The minimap overlays the actual route and next gate.

Arcade additions: rechargeable hold-to-boost (Shift / Boost), five mint boost pads, six gold mystery boxes, and three physical ramps per lap. Gold Rush supplies four seconds of boost; Executive Shield prevents rival bump drag for six seconds; Art of the Deal slows nearby rivals for four seconds. X or the item button uses the held item. Launches use gravity and preserve steering; landing restores some boost. Effects use simple shared geometry and instancing, without additional model downloads. AI drivers use pads and traverse ramps.

Extended validation covers the entire corridor at three lane positions against world solids, boost drain/recharge, each item effect/consumption, multiple ramp launches/landings, race recovery and the actual motor completing two boosted laps. Renderer remains stubbed; these are simulation checks, not a claim of hands-on mobile QA.
